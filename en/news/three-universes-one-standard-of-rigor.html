Three Universes, One Standard of Rigor

A novel that reads myths as memory, a board that thinks about soccer in real time, and an equation for measuring civilizations. What connects three books that seem to have nothing in common.

By Ellian Lessel·Published November 15, 2026·3 min read

Some books are written to answer a question, and others to ask it better. The three we are presenting today in this new home belong to the second kind. One travels six thousand years back, into the dust of Mesopotamia. Another stays for ninety minutes on the grass of a stadium. The third looks ahead, toward civilizations we may never meet. At first glance, they share nothing. And yet they were born of the same restlessness.

That restlessness is easy to state and hard to exhaust: how do complex systems think, decide, and endure? A people forced to leave its world is a complex system. So is a team of eleven players reorganizing itself every second. An entire species trying to survive its own technological power may be the most complex of all.

Memory beneath the dust

Ad Primordia — Volumen I: Despertar (“Awakening”; Lessel, 2026a) opens a five-volume saga. Its starting point is an intuition that any reader of ancient mythology has felt at some point: the stories of Sumer, Babylon, Assyria, and Persia resemble one another too closely. Floods, gods descending from the sky, wise figures who warn humanity, lost paradises. Coincidence, cultural inheritance, or something more?

The novel does not answer that question with an essay but with a story. In 2034, an archaeologist uncovers a six-sided temple beneath a mound on the Euphrates and, inside it, a voice that has waited six thousand years to be heard. From there, hard science fiction and comparative mythology move forward together.

Chess on the run

El tablero de césped (“The Turf Board”; Lessel, 2026b) grows out of a phrase we all repeat without examining it: “soccer is like chess.” The book takes that comparison seriously, so seriously that it turns it into a method. It divides the pitch into 64 squares, assigns coordinates, translates the pin, the fork, and zugzwang into the language of the game, and proposes a central idea, transmutation: in soccer, unlike chess, a piece can change its nature in the middle of a move.

It is not a recipe book. It is a vocabulary for seeing what was already there.

Measuring the immense

La eficiencia espaciotemporal (“Spacetime Efficiency”; Lessel, 2026c) takes issue with one of the most famous ideas in astrophysics: the Kardashev scale, which since 1964 has ranked civilizations by how much energy they control (Kardashev, 1964). The book proposes a different yardstick: not how much a civilization consumes, but how efficiently it does so. From that idea come six variables anchored in physical laws, a Master Equation, and a scale of six Grades. By current data, humanity barely reaches the first rung.

It is a theoretical proposal that presents itself as such: open to criticism, with predictions that could refute it. Its author does not ask to be believed, but to be put to the test.

What you will find in News

This space will not be a bulletin board. Every two weeks or every month, we will publish short, unhurried pieces on the territories the books explore: the mythologies of ancient civilizations, physics, astrophysics, and quantum mechanics, the search for intelligent life in the universe, and strategy in sport and in games. Some pieces will start from the books; others, from the questions readers send us.

There will be five sections: Publishing News, to follow the stage each book is in; Behind Ad Primordia, to explore the saga’s mythical and historical sources without revealing what has not yet been published; Strategy & Play; Science & Cosmos; and Reader Q&A, where we will take up, with permission, the messages you write to us.

That is what the reader mailbox, at the bottom of this section, is for. You can leave a message, a suggestion, a reading recommendation, or a topic you would like to see covered. Everything goes straight to the author.

An invitation

Three books, three doors. You do not need to walk through all of them. But whoever crosses one usually discovers that, on the other side, the hallway connects to the rest: the strategist finds myths, the reader of myths finds physics, the cosmos enthusiast finds a board.

All three books are written and published in Spanish.
